About

Careful engineering, explained plainly.

I'm a master's student moving into functional safety and robotics. My background is in sensors, estimation and simulation, and I'm now learning the safety lifecycle that sits around machines that work next to people.

I'm at the start of that road, and the projects below show it: every one is a learning project, labelled as such, with its assumptions and limitations written down. I'd rather show a small chain that is traceable and checked than a big claim I can't back up.

Quantum computing Simulator only

Qiskit quantum teleportation

Moves a qubit state using an entangled pair and two classical bits. A negative control that skips the corrections lands at a coin flip (about 0.5), which shows the corrections are what make it work. A noise sweep keeps success at 0.886 even at 10% two-qubit error.

The idea behind the cobot project

Every requirement traces back to a hazard and forward to a test.

In a safety case, nothing may float free. My cobot project keeps this chain in plain files and a script checks it for gaps. Press the button to see what a gap looks like.

  1. 112HazardsWhat can hurt someone, rated by severity, frequency and possibility to avoid
  2. 2PLrRequired levelRead off the ISO 13849-1 risk graph for each hazard
  3. 37Safety functionsEach targeted at PL d, category 3
  4. 420RequirementsWhat each function must do
  5. 520Test cases11 test, 5 measurement, 3 inspection, 1 analysis
  • Every safety function has a hazard behind it
  • Every requirement has a test case
  • No target PL is lower than the PL the hazards demand

All checks pass. The chain is complete.

Learning path

Standards I'm working through

I use public summaries to learn the structure. Any limit value I quote has to be checked against the purchased standard before real use, and my repos say so.

    Risk assessment and risk reduction

    Functional safety of E/E/PE systems
